    The problem

    Paying many suppliers is where money leaks

    Organisations in Uganda pay many service providers, large and small. The same three problems keep coming up, and they get worse once AI agents start making payments, because an agent can't tell who is really behind a payee.

    ?

    Ghost or duplicate suppliers

    Money sent to a payee nobody can identify, or to the same supplier twice.

    !

    Unauthorised payments

    Amounts above what was approved, or spending that runs past the budget.

    §

    Gaps at audit time

    Payments with no clear record of who approved them, who received them, and why.

    How it works

    Five checks on every payment

    If any rule fails, the agent refuses before a signature exists and says why.

    1

    Buyer verified

    The supplier agent only serves buyers with a Verified by Concordium badge.

    2

    Payee verified

    The buyer checks the payee owns an active, badged agent tied to a real identity.

    3

    Owner's limits

    Per-payment cap, daily limit, allowed assets and an approved-supplier list.

    4

    Sponsored payment

    Paid over x402 on Concordium. Kakasa's facilitator covers the network fee.

    5

    Receipt trail

    Both sides add a hash-chained receipt linking the payment to two verified owners.

    Payment locks

    Five operations, one human decision

    Every invoice payment goes through a lock. The agent does the checking; a person makes the call. Money moves once — on release — as a sponsored payment on Concordium.

    1

    Create

    The owner opens a lock for one supplier invoice.

    2

    Fund

    The amount is set aside against the day's budget. Over €10,000? Refused.

    3

    Send

    The AI agent checks the supplier's badge and every limit, and explains the result.

    A person approves by signing with the owner's wallet. Only now is the payment settled on-chain.

    Cancel

    EU AI Act

    Built around human oversight and records

    Kakasa prepares business-to-business supplier payments. That use isn't on the AI Act's high-risk list, but Cord applies the Act's core safeguards anyway, so organisations can deploy it with confidence.

    Art. 14

    Human oversight

    Release needs a person, who signs it with the owner's own Concordium wallet (browser or phone). The owner can cancel any lock or pause the agent instantly.

    Art. 12

    Record-keeping

    Every operation, refusal and policy change is logged automatically in a hash-chained audit log, alongside on-chain payment receipts.

    Art. 13 · 50

    Transparency

    Users are told they are working with an AI agent, and see its checks and reasons before they decide.

    Art. 9 · 15

    Risk controls

    Per-lock and daily limits (€10,000 in the demo), verified-supplier checks and refusal before signing keep errors from turning into payments.

    Art. 26

    Deployer duties

    Logs are kept for review (at least six months), and oversight is assigned to named people in the organisation.

    Identity

    Accountable agents

    Both agents carry a Verified by Concordium badge, so every payment links to two identity-verified owners.

    Timing: the AI Act's transparency duties (Art. 50) apply from 2 August 2026; after the 2026 Digital Omnibus, high-risk obligations for stand-alone systems apply from 2 December 2027. This page describes how Cord is designed; it is not legal advice.

    Beyond Concordium

    Suppliers on other blockchains welcome

    A supplier's agent doesn't have to live on Concordium. Agents on Ethereum, Base, Solana and other chains can link their key to a Concordium agent through the CIS-8 external key registry. Cord looks that link up and runs the same Verified by Concordium check before anyone is paid.

    1

    Link the key

    The supplier proves it controls its Ethereum or Solana key and links it to a Concordium agent owned by an identity-verified account.

    2

    Kakasa checks it

    Paste the supplier's Solana address or EVM public key — or let them sign once with MetaMask — and Cord finds the linked agent and badge.

    3

    Pay with confidence

    Approve the verified owner as a supplier. Payments still settle on Concordium, under the same limits and human release.

    Built on Concordium

    Under the hood

    Agent Registry · CIS-8004

    Each agent is an on-chain record pointing to its Agent Card, pinned by a SHA-256 hash.

    Verified by Concordium

    Ties every agent to an identity-verified owner account. Checked by both sides.

    x402 payments

    The supplier answers "402 Payment Required"; the buyer pays and retries in one flow.

    Sponsored transactions

    Kakasa's facilitator pays network fees, so small invoices stay cheap.

    Stablecoins · PLT

    Invoices priced in a stablecoin such as USDR at full value. On testnet, small CCD amounts.

    MCP

    An owner can instruct the purchasing agent through Claude or any MCP client.
